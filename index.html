06 — WHAT I CAUGHT

Five things this case caught

Small details, expensive consequences. Catching them is the job.

CATCH 01

Member ID — two digits swapped

She read her member ID aloud as BHP-4471-20983; the card says BHP-4471-20938. I billed from the card, digit by digit, and listed the mismatch for follow-up — billing the spoken version would have meant a denied claim.

CATCH 02

Address — an old one still on file

She moved in August 2026. The old address (310 Elm Court Dr) is marked outdated and never used on the claim; the chart and claim use 4821 Magnolia Ridge Dr, Apt 3B.

CATCH 03

Privacy — who can be told what

Her husband Jerome may be told about appointments and bills only — never test results. Voicemail is clinic name and callback number only.

CATCH 04 · URGENT

Allergy — amoxicillin vs penicillin

Amoxicillin is in the penicillin family and she is allergic to penicillin (hives) — but the pharmacy's own record showed “none on file”. Flagged URGENT and routed to Dr. Okafor first, before the two routine refills.

CATCH 05

Scope — what a VA must not do

I prepared the refill summaries and passed on the provider's message in plain words. I did not approve or deny any refill, change a dose, diagnose, or explain what a lab result means. Every decision box is left blank for the provider.

Explaining the bill to the patient

“Thank you for asking, Ms. Carter. The $30 you paid at check-in was your fixed visit fee, called a copay, and it covered part of the office visit only. Your lab tests are billed separately, and you still had $50 left to pay toward your yearly deductible — the amount you pay first before insurance shares the cost — so that $50, plus a small 20 percent share of the remaining lab cost, is why you owe $53.80 more, for a total of $83.80 for the visit. Please do not pay that extra amount yet. Wait for the statement from your insurance, called an Explanation of Benefits, which shows exactly what was paid, and call us if it does not match.”

Dr. Okafor — three refill requests from Riverside Pharmacy, 11/04/2026, for Rosalind Carter (DOB 06/22/1971):

  • UrgentAmoxicillin 500 mg: conflicts with her penicillin allergy (hives); pharmacy has no allergy on file. Please review first.

Full summaries attached. No decisions made by the VA — decision boxes are blank for you.

08 — REFLECTION · CASE STUDY

What this case taught me

This case taught me to work in order and check everything twice. For costs, I apply the copay first, then the remaining deductible on the lab lines in order, then 20% coinsurance — and I cross-check the totals both ways, charges minus write-offs and patient-paid plus plan-paid against the allowed amounts. For insurance, I copy details directly from the card, digit by digit, and list any mismatch for follow-up instead of guessing. And for refills, I prepare the summary and flag safety concerns to the provider — I never decide a refill myself.
